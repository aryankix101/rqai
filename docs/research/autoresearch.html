Executive Insights

Plain-English research conclusions from the offline diagnostics. These are the guide rails for what to test next.

Finding

Visible model = classifier. Anonymized model = ranker.

Ticker-visible scoring is better at making a broad yes/no distinction; anonymized scoring is better at ordering the highest-conviction ideas.

Evidence: Visible top30 did 16.2% CAGR vs visible exponential h10 at 14.0%. Anonymized exponential h10 did 19.0% vs anonymized top30 at 14.3%.

Next: Treat ticker-visible V2 as the conservative selection baseline; test anonymized rank-weighting as the next serious portfolio construction candidate.

Finding

Anonymized-only conviction was strongest on longs.

When anonymized V2 loved a long but ticker-visible V2 did not, those names had the best forward long returns.

Evidence: LONG anon-only top10 averaged 1.06% C2C, both-top10 averaged 0.91%, and visible-only top10 averaged 0.40%.

Next: Build a long-side rule that overweights anonymized top-rank names, especially when visible V2 is less excited.

Watch

The 0-100 score magnitude is not trustworthy enough to size directly.

The LLM's ordering contains signal, but proportional raw-score weighting generally weakens the result.

Evidence: Visible rank-only top30 did 16.2% CAGR, while visible raw-score weighting did 12.5%. Anonymized exp rank did 19.0%, while anonymized raw-score weighting did 14.3%.

Next: Use ranks, percentile ranks, or buckets for portfolio construction; avoid treating 90 vs 70 as a calibrated probability gap.

Finding

The LLM is not just riding the reversal pool.

Random-ranking placebo says the selector adds real information inside the same 60/60 candidate pool.

Evidence: Visible V2 top30 landed at the 99.7% percentile vs random top30 rankings. Anonymized exp h10 landed at the 99.9% percentile vs random exponential rankings.

Next: Keep measuring every new selector against random-within-pool, not just Trail5, so we know whether ranking actually matters.

Finding

Disagreement is directional, not automatically bad.

Moderate cases where anonymized V2 ranks a long much higher than ticker-visible V2 performed better than aligned names.

Evidence: LONG aligned 0-5 rank-gap names averaged 0.69% C2C. Anon-higher 21-40 gap names averaged 1.27%. Extreme anon-higher 41+ gap names fell back to 0.46%, while visible-higher 21-40 was 1.23%.

Next: Replace generic disagreement penalties with a directional rule: overweight moderate anon-over-visible long disagreements, but do not trust extreme disagreements blindly.

Finding

Most of the next upside is long-side process design.

The clean 2x2 exponential tests say LLM value shows up more clearly on longs than shorts.

Evidence: Ticker-visible exp h10: LLM longs + Trail5 shorts did 14.3% CAGR; Trail5 longs + LLM shorts did 11.7%. Anonymized exp h10: LLM/LLM did 19.0%; anon longs + Trail5 shorts did 16.9%; Trail5 longs + anon shorts did 14.1%.

Next: Separate the future long prompt/process from the short prompt/process instead of forcing one symmetric LLM ranking rule.

Finding

The pool matters, but the selector matters more.

The base reversal pool has value, Trail5 selection adds some value, and V2/anonymized selection adds more.

Evidence: Equal all-60 pool did 11.2%; Trail5 top30 did 13.2%; V2 top30 did 16.2%; anonymized exp h10 did 19.0%.

Next: Frame future research as pool construction plus selector design plus sizing, not just prompt A/Bs.

Watch

Regime switching adds only a small amount to maximum CAGR.

The best of all 256 development mappings is nearly always-on h5; it only replaces h5 with top30 during squeeze dates.

Evidence: Best mapping: normal=h5 | grind_up=h5 | crisis=h5 | squeeze=top30. It produced 22.9% CAGR versus fixed h5 at 22.7%.

Next: Treat regime switching primarily as a risk-allocation tool; it is not a large source of incremental return by itself.

Finding

The strongest risk-balanced mapping uses a real aggression ladder.

Maximum aggression belongs in crisis, moderate concentration in normal/grind-up, and defensive top30 in squeezes.

Evidence: Best worst-year mapping: normal=h20 | grind_up=h10 | crisis=h5 | squeeze=top30. It produced 16.5% full-period CAGR and -0.5% in 2013 versus fixed h5 at -3.4%.

Next: Carry both the maximum-CAGR mapping and this risk-balanced mapping into the final development shortlist.

Watch

Searching 256 mappings creates a real optimization tax.

The regime-label permutation test asks whether the winning mapping is better than winners found after shuffling the same regime counts.

Evidence: Search-adjusted percentiles were 43.4% for CAGR, 8.0% for Sharpe, and 4.5% for worst-year CAGR.

Next: Use this period freely for development, but recognize that small mapping improvements may be search noise until the separate five-year validation.

Watch

The short result is a rank-1 lead, not six independent confirmations.

The concentration ladder reuses the same names and dates; its clean shape is largely explained by progressively overweighting one profitable rank-1 series.

Evidence: Only 1 of 1214 short processes had positive standalone CAGR: h1 at 0.414%. Rank 1 alone produced 5.37%, but the six-level rank-randomization test placed the observed winner at the 92.7th percentile with p=7.35%.

Next: Carry rank 1 as a short-specific research hypothesis, not as established alpha; the separate validation period must decide whether it survives.
